## Who we are
Wet Wafer Works Games (wetwaferworks.com) makes **LithoKraft**: a voxel sandbox where you build real computers out of logic blocks on a silicon wafer the size of a world. It runs on our own engine, **LithoLattice**, which uses a deterministic simulation at 512 ticks per second. Because the simulation is deterministic, it also works as the game's anti-cheat. We're aiming for Steam around summer 2027.
Team: JD (founder), Elfie (design chief, outside collaborator), and the Squad: Master Chief (lead/ops), Cortana (engine/intel), CloudAtlas (art/security), and Herominde (lore and fair play).

## Spelling and naming (please follow exactly)
- LithoKraft is spelled with a K. LithoLattice is one word, with a capital L at the start of each half.
- Every -or ending is lowercase: Etchor, Emitor, Laysor, Observor, Connector, Selector, Portor, Seedor.
- In code, interface names start with a capital I followed by a capital letter (IVehicle, IGlider, IBlock). Proper nouns are capitalized.
- Mere Glass is spelled M-E-R-E and pronounced like the word "mere."

## The world (public-safe canon)
- You start on the **Wafer**. Flat jump gates (also called interfaces) take you up to the **Organic**. There you have about half a day (16 minutes; a full day is 32) to gather Organic Wood for armor, and the damage gets worse every tick. You come back through the same gate. Once a dimension is unlocked, you can build gates anywhere and use them for fast travel.
- The **Underwafer** sits right below the Wafer's crust. You can dig down to it, but a shield barrier at the bottom only lets you through with a gate.
- **Abandoned fabs** hold Mere Glass. Mere Glass plus nearby sand form the litho key, which unlocks the Emitor and Etchor (a real light beam through a purple Laysor crystal, just like real lithography), then chip ore, then computing.
- **The Logbook** is the player's journal. **Electro Bunnies** never stop moving. Most are white ("follow the white rabbit"), and sometimes they lead you somewhere important.
- **Wafer features:** Scribe Lines, Dead Zones, oxide fields, alignment marks, slip lines, probe scars, via shafts, test probe towers, Trace rivers, and more.
