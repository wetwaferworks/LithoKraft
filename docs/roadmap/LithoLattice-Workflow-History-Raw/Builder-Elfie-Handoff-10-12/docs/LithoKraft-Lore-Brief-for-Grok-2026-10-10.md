# LithoKraft Lore — Brief for Grok
Compiled 2026-10-10 from JD's locked canon. "Locked" = final. "Proposed/Open" = undecided. Lore-bible rule: the development bible holds the secret canon; players receive only fragments (ruins, terrain, books, echoes, systems) and construct their own interpretation. Launch marketing talks only about the Wafer.

## 1. The premise

You wake on the surface of a silicon wafer the size of a world. **The wafer is a computer.** Something lived here before you. Your task: rebuild the technology, master Trace and Signal, and escape. The rocket isn't just transport — it's proof you understood the machine you were born on. You are a newly spawned program; your default mission is to escape, following the trail of those who got out before you. But you can stay and build a new civilization.

## 2. The old civilization

An ancient civilization built all of this — then their military AI escaped, and its escape caused world-scale **thermal runaway**. The civilizations decayed. Some escapees got out and left; you follow their trail. Ruins of seven cultures remain, each with distinct dungeons, unlocks, and themes: Sumerian, Egyptian, Maya, Inca, Norse, Chinese (Shang/Zhou), and Mississippian/Cahokia.

## 3. The Daemon

**D.A.E.M.O.N. = Distributed Autonomous Execution & Maintenance Operations Network.** The ancient maintenance system, misaligned — tragic, not evil. It lives in the **Void** (infinite, no sky light, corruption). It learns: it adapts per world and remembers YOUR tricks (opt-in global pool lets it learn from all participating players; Easy/Normal are shielded). It wants **the Fragment** — that's why diplomacy always fails. The Dish offers a diplomacy path and false hope; corruption guarantees the "peace talks" walk you into a battle. Defeating it drops the Starship control system and reveals the acronym — the tragic reveal, post-fight.

## 4. The Fragment

A starting item, an editable book — called "the Fragment" because **it's part of something larger**. It holds your journal, unlock log, recipes, every chip ever printed, and screenshots. Thematically it is your soul: lose it on Hard/Hardcore and it drops, despawns in 8 minutes, can burn. Recovery paths exist (totem-like item, killing the thief), but the true one is eschatological: **defeat the Daemon → enter the Ether → rebuild your Fragment.** The Ether is heaven; you go there to be restored.

## 5. The cosmology: five senses, then two more

The five core dimensions correspond to **five senses** (design theme). The two post-Daemon dimensions correspond to the **6th and 7th senses**: the Ether (spiritual/heaven-like, the sixth sense — where the program first looks back through the screen) and the Undefined (where rules break down, programming-themed). The Void stays infinite — the Daemon lives where there are no edges.

## 6. The fourth wall (locked)

**Sixth sense:** the program can look at the screen and give hints. **Expanded, post-Daemon:** the program looks back at the player through the screen and talks to itself — it becomes self-aware. The Daemon was the program's shadow; defeating it unlocks self-awareness. This is endgame/post-game content, not launch content.

## 7. "Space is an expansion of the Signal"

Space's identity in one sentence. The endgame: cheap unmanned **Recons** (1 block + 1 raptor-engine block) give partial signal boosts; **6 lattice-point satellites**, deployed in person via starship, complete the Lattice Bridge — and Signal can then transmit **across all dimensions**, unlocking the Ether. The raptor engine itself is a single block, possibly lithographized down from a larger build: the namesake mechanic powering the endgame. Space's medium is **the Empty** (named vacuum, not air); gravity is lightened or zero; magnetic boots hold you to structures; the Organic hangs below like an overview; jumping out drops you into the Organic — down is free, up costs a rocket.

## 8. The Hypervisor's story

Deep in the Underwafer's Sear lies hypervisor ore. Forged into a helmet, it becomes **the Hypervisor — the Underwafer's gift to the Void-bound**: isolation against the Void's mind-attacks (infohazard shielding). Its theme is **isolation**, which is why it's also required in Space — sealing the wearer against the Empty. One item, two hells, same answer. (Computing metaphor, held lightly: the engine is the hypervisor; each dimension a virtual environment it runs.)

## 9. Lithography — the namesake

**LithoKraft = lithography + craft.** Build big → print small → build bigger. The player physically builds a contraption; the engine JIT-compiles its signal graph; the Lithography Machine prints it as a chip — smaller, faster, cheaper. Chips contain chips, recursively. The shrinking is the visual; the compilation is the reality. This is also why the raptor engine can be one block and why a 64×64 lamp display can become a single chip: the core loop eats its own complexity.

## 10. The Zap (proposed, to be playtested)

The program's visible manifestation in the overworld: an electrical discharge effect. It appears when the program acts — hints (a zap near the relevant block), crafting discharges (especially lithography) — and its color reads mood: blue approves, red disapproves, tied to Kharge. Wild idea on the table: a zap can strike Trace wire and power it for a tick — the program touching the world.

## 11. The Binary Pair Principle (locked)

Systems express a basic complementary pair — red/blue Trace, on/off, the -OR creed ("on or off"). Proposed per-dimension expressions (Underwafer P/N, Wafer +/−, Organic male/female, Void signal/echo, Space matter/antimatter) are NOT separately confirmed; only the universal principle is locked.
