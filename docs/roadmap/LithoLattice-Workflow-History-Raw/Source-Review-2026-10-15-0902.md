## PRIORITY FINDING: mouse never re-captures after alt-tab (confirmed by JD, Release, Windows)

**Root cause** (game side, not GLFW):
1. `game/LithoKraft/src/app/main.cpp:887-890`: when focus is lost, `game.onFocusChanged(false)` runs.
2. `game/LithoKraft/src/game/game.cpp:1369-1370` (`Game::onFocusChanged`): when you lose focus while Playing, this calls `setPaused(true)`, which moves to `PauseState::MenuPaused`, opens the **Paused dialog** (`game.cpp:1349-1351`) and pushes the Inventory input context.
3. `game/LithoKraft/src/game/include/lithokraft/game/game.h:138`: `wantsCursorCaptured()` is `captured_ && !inventoryOpen_ && !editorOpen() && !paused_`. While paused it is always false, so `main.cpp:890/893` keeps the cursor `GLFW_CURSOR_NORMAL`.
4. `game/LithoKraft/src/game/game.cpp:1776-1777`: `clickUsed = uiClick || inventoryOpen_ || editorOpen() || paused_;`. While paused, **every world click is swallowed**. Click-to-grab also only sets `captured_` (which is already true) and never unpauses. And because the Inventory context is pushed, `Action::Break` is gated anyway.
5. The only way out is the dialog's **Resume** button (`game_ui.cpp:134`, wired to `setPaused(false)` at `game.cpp:50`) or Esc being taken by the modal. JD clicks into the 3D view, not the Resume button, so the click does nothing and the cursor stays free. To him it reads as "never re-captures". At about 7 FPS on the software renderer, and with a dark menu overlay, the dialog is easy to miss.

**Contributing defects:**
- `main.cpp:836/887` detects focus edges by polling `glfwGetWindowAttrib(GLFW_FOCUSED)` once per frame. At 7 FPS (about 134 ms per frame), a quick alt-tab away and back can fall entirely between two polls. The game then never sees the change, and its cached `Window::captured_` (`window.cpp:178-181`) can disagree with GLFW's real state. Line 893 compares against that cached flag, so it never re-applies. The `FocusLost/FocusGained` events that `glfw_input_backend.cpp:120-124` already queues are only used by `DeviceState::releaseAll` (`device_state.cpp:68-69`) and never reach the game.
- `game.cpp:1304-1310` `handlePause()`: Esc while already paused calls `setPaused(true)` again (it never toggles). It only works because the modal dialog usually takes Esc first.

**Suggested fix (no code changed):**
- (a) Drive focus from the queued `FocusLost/FocusGained` events, not frame polling, so no edge is lost at low FPS.
- (b) Pick one of these behaviours (JD's call). Either don't auto-pause on focus loss: just release the cursor and recapture on the first click in the window (Minecraft Java style). Or keep auto-pause, but let a click in the world area outside the dialog act as Resume. In `game.cpp:1776` that means: if `pauseState_==MenuPaused` and the pause was caused by focus loss and the click wasn't consumed by the UI, call `setPaused(false)`.
- (c) In `main.cpp:893`, compare against `glfwGetInputMode(win, GLFW_CURSOR)` (the real state), not the cached `captured_`, and re-apply on `FocusGained`.
- (d) Make Esc in `handlePause()` toggle: when `pauseState_==MenuPaused`, call `setPaused(false)`.

Determinism: none (presentation/input only).

## HIGH

**H1. Meshing gets slower with more threads: root cause.** `engine/src/world/include/litholattice/world/block.h:717,725` (`has()` → `def()` → `reg_->blockDef()`, which takes a `std::shared_lock` on `defMutex_`, `block.h:352/510/640`), called per voxel from `render/mesher.cpp:109` (5,832 calls per sub-chunk just to fill `opaque[]`) and throughout the face loop, from `mesh_input.cpp` (`findByStableId`), and from `binaryMeshSubChunk`. Every reader does an atomic read-modify-write on the same SRWLOCK/shared_mutex cache line. With 12 mesh threads, that line bounces between cores on every voxel, so throughput drops as threads are added (the measured result was about 4× slower on 12 threads than on 1). The sim thread also calls `blocks_.has` on every change (`sim_thread.cpp:395,404`), so meshing also adds jitter to sim ticks.
*Fix:* at the top of each mesh job, take ONE shared lock or an immutable `shared_ptr<const DefTable>` generation snapshot and build a flat flags LUT indexed by BlockId. Do all per-voxel lookups through the LUT. The existing epoch guard in `meshSubChunk` then becomes the only consistency check. Determinism: no (render only). Expected effect: linear scaling.

**H2. Software renderer: no near-plane clipping. Triangles straddling the camera are dropped.** In `software_renderer.cpp:91-110`, `transformVertex` keeps the sign of w, and callers reject on w ≤ 0. Without clipping, any quad crossing the near plane (blocks you're standing next to or inside) is thrown out whole, leaving holes close to the camera. *Fix:* clip in homogeneous space against w=near (Sutherland–Hodgman on one plane) before the divide. Determinism: no.

**H3. Software renderer rasterizer cost (the alpha focus).** `software_renderer.cpp:146-178` and `217-304`:
- 3 float divisions per pixel (`/area`) across the whole bounding box, including pixels outside the triangle.
- No incremental edge stepping, no tiles or threads (single-threaded), and no early-out once a row has been exited.
- u/v/world-position interpolation is affine in screen space (not perspective-correct, so textures swim up close).
- The ground-pulse branch rebuilds params structs per fragment.
*Fix:* precompute `invArea` once; do incremental integer or fixed-point edge functions with a top-left fill rule; bin triangles into 64×64 tiles and rasterize the tiles on the JobPool; interpolate 1/w-weighted varyings; hoist `GroundPulseParams`/`GroundTraceParams` out of the pixel loop. This is the main lever on the measured 134 ms per frame. Determinism: no.

**H4. Game thread blocks on `blockMutex_` for a whole sim burst.** `sim_thread.cpp:565-634` holds `blockMutex_` across up to 16 master steps (catch-up). Every `SimThread::getBlock()` from the game thread (projectile voxel query `game.cpp:82`, collision `game.cpp:1822`, liquid test `game.cpp:1850`) waits for the whole burst. At 512 TPS with catch-up, that can be several ms per call, several times per frame, which shows up as frame hitches. *Fix:* release and re-acquire the lock between master steps inside the `while (acc_ >= stepDur_)` loop. Or better, serve game-thread reads from a published per-tick read view (a seqlock, or the copy-on-write chunk list from the streaming design). Determinism: no (reads only), as long as edits still go through the control stream.

**H5. Hold-path catch-up burst after a stream insert (today's fix).** `game.cpp:928-993` (diff vs `.orig`) and `sim_thread.cpp:583-586`. The old stop()/start() re-based `last` in `run()`. With `holdBetweenTicks`, the sim thread blocks on the mutex while `acc_` keeps growing from wall time. After release it runs up to 16 steps immediately and drops anything past 100 ms (`dropped_`). Simulation results are unchanged (ticks are discrete and edits are tick-stamped), but you get a burst of ticks right after each insert, which also prolongs H4. *Fix:* keep the insert budget small (it is 4 columns now, which is fine). Optionally have `holdBetweenTicks` record the hold duration and subtract it from `acc_`, so held time counts as paused time. Determinism: no (tick count per wall-second only).

## MEDIUM

**M1. Hard-coded Linux paths not ported.** `game/LithoKraft/src/app/interchange_capture.cpp:126,177,178` (`/home/hatch/workspace/litholattice/...`). `biome_capture.cpp` was fixed today with a search list (`biome_capture.cpp:64-87`), but interchange_capture still fails on Windows. This likely explains some of the "file paths that point at Elfie's machine" test failures. *Fix:* reuse biome_capture's root finder. Determinism: no.

**M2. Handshake workers wake on every publish, even with no due work.** In `timing_handshake.cpp:546-556`, every `runPhase` (one group-2 per master step plus Signal's group-1 phases) bumps `publishSeq_` and calls `notify_all`. Every worker wakes, takes `m_`, marks its done-seq, and then yield-spins for 50 µs (`:498`) before blocking again. At about 2.5k publishes per second that is roughly 10–15% of a core per worker, taken from the mesh pool and the software renderer on the 8-core laptop. *Fix:* when `activeShards==0`, publish without `notify_all` (or skip the publish entirely if no worker needs the seq). Shrink the spin window, or make it adaptive. Determinism: no.

**M3. Per-phase heap allocation.** `timing_handshake.cpp:519-520` allocates `std::vector<Comp*> affine` and `std::vector<char> shardActive` on every `runPhase` (thousands per second on the authority thread). *Fix:* make them members and `clear()` them each phase. Determinism: no.

**M4. Stale-worker diagnostics race.** `timing_handshake.cpp:522-525` resets `started/finished/durationNs` for every component with relaxed stores, while a worker from a previous frozen or stale generation may still be inside `runItem` writing them. This only affects watchdog naming and stats (not world state), but the freeze report can name the wrong component. *Fix:* reset only due components, or tag the stamps with the generation. Determinism: no.

**M5. Windows private-key protection is weaker than on POSIX.** `crypto/signing.cpp:173-176`: on Windows, `std::filesystem::permissions` only toggles the read-only attribute and sets no ACL. There is also no symlink refusal (no `O_NOFOLLOW` equivalent). Today's key is protected only by the folder ACL JD set by hand. *Fix:* create the file with an explicit owner-only DACL (`CreateFileW` with `SECURITY_ATTRIBUTES`) and refuse reparse points. Also, `std::random_device` (`signing.cpp:124`) is non-deterministic on MSVC/clang-cl but was deterministic on older MinGW, so assert the toolchain or use `BCryptGenRandom` directly. Determinism: no.

**M6. The release key is a dev key.** Per `Hitching-Diagnosis-2026-10-15-0807.md`, the baked key `04c1766e…011c` is a local dev key that now passes the release FATAL check (`main.cpp:205-215`). That's fine for alpha, but there is now no guard that stops a dev-key Release from shipping. *Fix:* add a `kBakedKeyIsDevKey` flag that warns loudly in Release and blocks a `-DLITHO_SHIP=ON` build. Determinism: no.

**M7. `takeChangedColumns()` has no lock.** `sim_thread.h:188` says "call only while stopped". `saveWorld` does call it after stop() (`game.cpp:869-873`), but nothing enforces that. Now that the stream path uses `holdBetweenTicks` instead of stop(), a future caller may assume holding is enough, and `markDirty` (sim thread) inserts into the same `unordered_set`. *Fix:* take `blockMutex_` inside `takeChangedColumns()`. Determinism: no (but it affects save completeness).

## LOW

- **L1.** `sim_thread.cpp:633-638`: `blockMutex_` is released and immediately re-acquired between the tick section and the post section. That is intended (it gives the stream hand-off a window), but on Windows `std::recursive_mutex` isn't fair, so the hand-off can starve. Use an explicit "hold requested" flag that the sim thread honours at that point.
- **L2.** `sim_thread.cpp:148-159,230-236`: the `stepRefusalLogged_`/`freezeReported_` resets are correct (A-M5), no issue. Noting it as verified.
- **L3.** `core/chase_lev.h:75-77,145-146`: the slot is written and read non-atomically while a thief may read it before the CAS. This is the classic Chase–Lev formal data race (UB under the C++ model, benign on x86 with trivially copyable T). If T is `std::function`, a thief copying while the owner overwrites after wraparound is a real hazard. It's bounded by fixed capacity plus the full check, so it's low. Store a pointer or index rather than the job object.
- **L4.** `core/spsc_queue.h:45-47`: `items_` sits next to `head_` with no padding before it, so there's possible false sharing between the producer's item write and the consumer's `tail_`. Minor.
- **L5.** `software_renderer.cpp:197-211`: `faceColor` strip tint is "reddish" for both strip modes (documented simplification). Flat-mode captures can't tell red_via from blue_via. This may be related to the open **red_via/blue_via side_* faces** item if that was judged from flat captures. Verify with per-pixel captures.
- **L6.** `crypto/signing.cpp:137-139`: `SecretKey sec` and the seed stay on the stack and are never wiped. Use a secure zero.
- **L7.** `crypto/u128.h`: the MSVC struct reproduces exactly the operators used, and `mul64`, carry and shifts are correct and bit-identical. Adding `static_assert` tests against known SHA-512/Ed25519 vectors under MSVC would lock this in (the crypto ctest already passes). Verified, no defect.
