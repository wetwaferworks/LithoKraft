# Research — Possible Phase 4: Streaming & Culling for Litho Voxel / Litho Texture

**Origin:** advisory text JD pasted into chat at 05:09 EDT naming two cautions — (1) *Page Table Thrashing* under rapid camera movement on a 4 GB card, judged by 1% / 0.1% lows; (2) *Traversal & Culling Overhead* at high frame rates, keeping voxel passes lean with aggressive frustum and occlusion culling. ## 1. The two failure modes, stated precisely

**1.1 Streaming thrash under fast traversal.** Average FPS can look excellent while the worst frames collapse. A whip-pan or high-speed traversal crosses chunk borders and demands new tiles and meshes *while* old ones are being evicted — uploads, tile-mapping updates, and meshing requests burst into the same few frames. On a 4 GB card the working set genuinely does not fit, so **eviction policy and upload pacing decide smoothness**, not raw throughput. The metric that convicts is the frame-time distribution — 1% and 0.1% lows — never the average.

**1.2 Traversal & culling overhead.** At 130–160 FPS the entire frame budget is 6–8 ms, so culling and meshing must be a bounded, small slice of it. If culling cost scales with the *total* chunk count instead of the visible set, or if meshing bursts land on the frame path, spikes appear exactly when scene complexity scales — the moment the engine can least afford them.

## 2. Findings — streaming & residency (the Litho Texture side)

1. **Hardware provides mechanism, not policy.** Sparse textures perform address translation in silicon, but they do not decide which pages load, when they evict, or how feedback is generated — engines implement their own virtual-texturing policy on top. (Source: [How Virtual Textures Really Work](https://www.shlom.dev/articles/how-virtual-textures-really-work/))
2. **Vulkan sparse residency is built for this shape.** Images created with `VK_IMAGE_CREATE_SPARSE_RESIDENCY_BIT` can be partially resident, bound in pixel-extent blocks, and populated only in the regions currently used — the spec documentation explicitly describes the megatexture use and unbinding whole mip levels under memory pressure. (Source: [Vulkan spec — Sparse Resources](https://vulkan.lunarg.com/doc/view/1.4.321.1/windows/antora/spec/latest/chapters/sparsemem.html); overview: [Sawicki, Vulkan sparse binding](https://www.asawicki.info/news_1698_vulkan_sparse_binding_-_a_quick_overview))
3. **Cap the per-frame streaming work — this is the single most repeated practice.** NVIDIA's RTX Texture Streaming guidance: limit the maximum number of tiles mapped per frame to smooth streaming, batch feedback resolve/clear calls, and consider stochastic feedback writes. Unreal's virtual-texture practice is the same shape: a max-uploads-per-frame knob and a pool sized so it does not thrash; forum reports confirm the failure mode appears "when moving/turning/teleporting the camera quickly." (Sources: [RTXTS texture streaming mechanism](https://deepwiki.com/NVIDIA-RTX/RTXTS/2.1-texture-streaming-mechanism); [Unreal RVT forum thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/runtime-virtual-texture-on-landscape-blur-issues/2838608); [Polycount VT discussion](https://polycount.com/discussion/comment/2789053#Comment_2789053))
4. **Make the streaming rate adaptive, and bias mips while the camera is fast.** The Fraunhofer adaptive-streaming work adjusts the per-frame streaming load from recent frame-latency measurements (spreading spikes across frames instead of absorbing them in one) and applies an adaptive mip bias under fast camera motion — higher mips are exponentially more expensive to stream, and during a whip-pan nobody can perceive top-mip detail anyway. Bias relaxes as the camera settles. (Source: [High Performance, Adaptive Texture Streaming and Rendering of Large 3D Cities (Fraunhofer)](https://www.fraunhofer.sg/content/dam/singapur/documents/Publications/20210601%20High%20Performance,%20Adaptive%20Texture%20Streaming%20and%20Rendering%20of%20Large%203D%20Cities.pdf))
5. **Uploads live off the frame path.** The standard Vulkan streaming architecture: file I/O and staging on worker threads, GPU copies and layout transitions on a dedicated transfer queue, a timeline semaphore so graphics waits only on "latest finished upload," descriptor updates only at safe points. (Source: [Vulkan docs — Synchronization and Streaming](https://docs.vulkan.org/tutorial/latest/Building_a_Simple_Engine/Advanced_Topics/Synchronization_and_Streaming.html))
6. **Preallocate big, subdivide — never allocate in the frame path.** A field fix for a shipped game's traversal stutter found the cause in hundreds of small GPU allocation requests per frame creating CPU–GPU synchronization bottlenecks; preallocating large blocks and subdividing cut a 106 ms spike to ~13 ms (modder-reported) and roughly doubled 1% lows in independent testing. This is LithoKraft's own arena doctrine applied to textures: one residency atlas/arena, tiles sub-allocated, zero allocation during frames. (Sources: [WCCFTech — Ishimura Patch tests](https://wccftech.com/dead-space-remakes-infamous-traversal-stutters-finally-tamed-by-ishimura-patch-mod-our-tests-show-176-higher-0-1-lows/); [Gaming Ideology — patch explainer](https://www.gamingideology.com/dead-space-remake-ishimura-patch-mod-fixes-pc-traversal-stutter/))
7. **Prefetch along the motion, in priority order, placeholders first.** Streaming practice across engines: predictive prefetch from player position/velocity and look direction; priority queues (needed-now ahead of likely-soon ahead of background); low-res placeholder shown immediately, refined in place — players notice *absence*, not a brief soft mip. (Sources: [Stop the Stutter — Game Dev Assembly](https://gamedevnewsletter.substack.com/p/stop-the-stutter); [C4 Engine troubleshooting notes](https://www.mindfulchase.com/explore/troubleshooting-tips/game-development-tools/c4-engine-troubleshooting-at-scale-performance,-stability,-and-determinism.html))
8. **Vulkan gives no paging safety net — the census budget is the strategy.** Unlike D3D12, Vulkan exposes no budget/priority heap system; allocations are the application's, and any driver paging is outside the app's control. Practical consequence: IHwGpu's hardware census must hand the engine a VRAM working-set budget (with headroom for OS/compositor), and the engine degrades mip detail rather than exceed it. On the 4 GB profile this budget — not the card's total — is the number Phase 4 designs to. (Source: [Khronos forums — memory residency and Vulkan](https://community.khronos.org/t/about-memory-residency-and-vulkan-api/105635))

**Design consequence for Litho Texture:** Archie's decoupled design already helps — procedural tile evaluation means many "tiles" are *computed* rather than streamed, and the optional 4096² residency atlas is a natural single arena. The thrash risk concentrates in the residency atlas and chunk-mesh uploads; both get the same governor: per-frame caps, adaptive rate, prefetch, arena-only allocation.

## 3. Findings — traversal, culling, meshing (the Litho Voxel side)

1. **GPU-driven two-phase culling is the established pattern, and it composes with the mega-draw.** Phase A: compute frustum-culls instances/chunks (AABB vs. the 6 frustum planes) and emits draw arguments; a hierarchical Z-buffer (HZB) is built; Phase B: occlusion-tests the remainder against the HZB and appends the newly visible. Output lands in the same indirect-draw argument buffers the arena mega-draw already consumes — culling becomes a producer for Litho Voxel's single dispatch, not a parallel system. (Sources: [AZDO core techniques](https://deepwiki.com/potato3d/azdo/2-core-azdo-techniques); [Adria GPU-driven rendering](https://deepwiki.com/mateeeeeee/Adria/3.2-gpu-driven-rendering); [Chord Nanite-style culling](https://deepwiki.com/qiutang98/chord/3.2-nanite-style-mesh-pipeline:-culling-and-rasterization); lineage: Haar & Aaltonen, *GPU-Driven Rendering Pipelines*, SIGGRAPH 2015; Wihlidal, *Optimizing the Graphics Pipeline with Compute*, GDC 2016, via [foliage overdraw survey](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-05-11-foliage-overdraw))
2. **Two-stage visibility handles whip-pans honestly.** Draw what was visible last frame against the fresh HZB, rebuild the HZB, then draw the newly-visible set. Fast pans degrade to one frame of conservative visibility instead of a stall — the correct failure shape for T1 below. (Source: [Chord, two-stage visibility](https://deepwiki.com/qiutang98/chord/3.2-nanite-style-mesh-pipeline:-culling-and-rasterization))
3. **Voxel chunks are the friendly granularity.** A chunk's AABB frustum test is trivially cheap, and a per-chunk HZB test at coarse mips is conservative: the worst case is drawing a chunk that turns out hidden (some overdraw), never a hole in the world. Start per-chunk; go finer (sub-chunk groups) only if benches convict per-chunk overdraw.
4. **Meshing and uploading are a budgeted queue, never a burst.** Voxel-engine practice (Godot Voxel Tools' documentation describes it plainly): uploads proceed on the frame path only until a time slice elapses, then continue next frame; the queue is prioritized — near, in-frustum, and in the direction of travel first — with hysteresis so a chunk oscillating at the border does not re-mesh forever. (Source: [Voxel Tools — Performance](https://voxel-tools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/performance/))
5. **Visibility is set algebra — reuse it.** A working voxel renderer frames it as intersections (explored ∩ streamed ∩ visible). LithoKraft's streamer already tracks received/total per chunk; culling should consume that same state rather than maintain a second truth. (Source: [Rendering 100,000+ Blocks Without Melting Your GPU](https://www.fernandobaz.com/blog/rendering-100000-blocks-without-melting-your-gpu))
6. **Raymarching — registrar's flag.** The advisory assumes "voxel raymarching or chunk-meshing passes." LithoKraft's shipping paths are meshed; whether *any* current path raymarches (software renderer? a lighting pass?) must be convicted before Phase 4 budgets for empty-space skipping or step caps. If none does, that line of the advisory closes as not-applicable. (Open question §6.2.)
7. **Culling never touches the sim.** Culling consumes camera state and last frame's depth only. It must not feed back into the deterministic simulation — the same rule already locked for the logic-state bitbuffer. Render-side only, always.

## 4. Measurement doctrine — how Phase 4 would prove itself

- **Every number labeled** (the desk's standing rule, extended): build counter, game Alpha, display mode, GPU + VRAM, chunk count, scene/path, cache state (cold/warm). Averages never reported alone.
- **Report the distribution:** average FPS, 1% low, 0.1% low, and the worst-frame list with timestamps. Lows are computed differently by different tools — compare only within one tool/method. (Source: [What are 1% lows — Frametide](https://medium.com/@support_34727/what-are-1-lows-and-why-they-matter-more-than-average-fps-frametide-7cc670962474))
- **Torture tests (scripted, repeatable paths — JD's census method):**
  - **T1 Whip-pan:** fixed scene, scripted maximum-speed 180° pans, cold cache and warm cache runs.
  - **T2 Traversal sprint:** scripted high-speed traversal (glider/elytra-class speed) across many chunk borders, on the 4 GB profile with the working set deliberately larger than budget, forcing continuous evict + stream.
  - **T3 Complexity scale:** the T2 path repeated at 32 / 64 / 128 chunks to find where culling and meshing costs bend.
  - Method model: the traversal-trek benchmark style — a ~30-second fast trek designed to trigger streaming stutters, judged on 1% / 0.1% lows. (Source: [WCCFTech](https://wccftech.com/dead-space-remakes-infamous-traversal-stutters-finally-tamed-by-ishimura-patch-mod-our-tests-show-176-higher-0-1-lows/))
- **Counters (macro-gated logging doctrine — ON in debug, OFF in release):** tiles mapped/unmapped per frame; upload bytes per frame; evictions per frame; meshing queue depth and ms spent; chunks tested vs. culled by frustum and by occlusion; residency-arena occupancy vs. budget. Counts convict; impressions don't.

## 5. Proposed Phase 4 shape (candidate — JD locks scope, order, pen)

- **4.0 Instrumentation first.** Frame-time percentile capture + the §4 counters; zero behavior change. *Gate:* counters agree with an external capture on the same run. (JD's diagnostic doctrine: instrument, then convict.)
- **4.1 Streaming governor (Litho Texture).** Per-frame caps on uploads and tile mappings; adaptive streaming rate driven by recent frame latency; velocity-aware mip bias (bias up while angular/linear camera speed is high, relax on settle); predictive prefetch along the velocity vector; residency atlas as a sub-allocated arena; census-driven VRAM budgets with an explicit 4 GB profile.
- **4.2 GPU culling into the mega-draw (Litho Voxel).** Compute frustum pass per chunk writing the indirect arguments; HZB occlusion as phase B if 4.2's first step leaves a measured gap.
- **4.3 Meshing budget queue.** Time-sliced meshing/upload with priority (near → in-frustum → direction of travel) and border hysteresis.
- **Order note:** 4.0 precedes everything. Whether 4.1 or 4.2 goes second is decided by which failure the T1/T2 baselines convict first — measurement chooses, not preference.
