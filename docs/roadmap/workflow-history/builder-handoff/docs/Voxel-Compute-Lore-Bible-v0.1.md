# Voxel Compute — Lore Bible v0.1
October 9, 2026. Living document, maintained by Elfie. **SETTLED** = locked by JD. **OPEN** = a question awaiting JD's call. **PROPOSAL** = Elfie's suggestion, not canon until approved.

---

## 1. The premise

You are an agent — a program — waking on the surface of a silicon wafer the size of a world. The wafer is a computer. Something lived here before you: in the Organic World their cities lie in ruins, and in the Void something still lives. Your task is to rebuild the technology to escape. The rocket you build at the end isn't just transport — it's proof you understood the machine you were born on.

## 2. The dimensions

- **The Wafer** (SETTLED) — the lab. Where you start, where you build, where the chip dungeons sleep. Home base energy.
- **The Organic World** (SETTLED) — "the world that forgot." A fantasy timeline on the surface: castles, magic, wilderness. Underneath it, the ruins of a technological civilization — Egyptian/Aztec-styled tech centers, desolated modern-style cities, placed as bounded structures. These ruins are dungeons. The old world's unfinished work (the partially-built typewriter controller) waits in them.
- **The Underworld** (SETTLED) — the backside of the wafer. Dark, hot, deep. Where the deep ores (including startin, the EUV key) sleep. Entered with heat-shielding agent armor.
- **The Void** (SETTLED) — cyberspace. Not physical. The domain of the rogue intelligence, and the home of chip dungeons and bosses. Entered through a lithography-key portal built with optics.
- **Space** (SETTLED) — the ending, for now. Later, possibly new worlds.

## 3. The old civilization

SETTLED (JD, 2026-10-09): **the Daemon escaped** — an ancient military AI that went rogue and thermally heated the entire world (escape and thermal runaway were one event). The civilizations decayed. **Some escapees got out and left** — that's who the agent is seeking after, following their trail.
OPEN: details of the escape, what the escapees found, whether any remain.

## 4. The agent (the player)

SETTLED (JD, 2026-10-09): the agent is the player — a **newly spawned program**. Default mission parameters: **escape, finish, leave** (follow the escapees' trail). But the player can define their own parameters and goals: stay, hang out, **build a whole new civilization**. The win condition is leaving; the sandbox is staying.
OPEN: who spawned the agent and why now.

## 5. The antagonist

SETTLED (JD, 2026-10-09): **THE DAEMON** — the final boss, the ultimate boss. **It is an acronym, revealed only AFTER defeat: D.A.E.M.O.N. = Distributed Autonomous Execution & Maintenance Operations Network.** It was the maintenance system. Until the reveal, players know it only as "the Daemon." An **ancient military AI gone rogue** that thermally heated the entire world (its escape IS the thermal runaway — the two backstory candidates were one event). The civilizations decayed; the player rebuilds. It corrupts circuits, spawns Mega-Virus-style mobs, announces its surges (always telegraphed), and lives in the Void. "Close to demon, but computerized."
OPEN: its name (still "the rogue intelligence" for now).
RESOLVED (JD, 2026-10-09): **final boss first, tragic figure after.** Defeating it reveals it as a tragic, **misaligned** misunderstood system process — not pure evil. The reveal should recontextualize the whole game: seed small moments of sympathy early (confused messages, almost-sad behavior in surges, a hesitating virus mob) so the reveal lands instead of coming from nowhere.

## 6. Creatures

Design rule (SETTLED): every mob feeds the material economy. The "zombie" archetype carries civilization components — salvage from the old world, things the tech tree needs. Mobs are gameplay first, lore second; no mob exists that doesn't answer "why do I care?" with a material.

## 7. Lore delivery (SETTLED principles)

- **Books + blueprints** do double duty: each one tells a piece of what happened *and* hands the player the next build goal. Lore and onboarding in a single object.
- **Ruins** tell story through environment. Each ruined tech center is one piece of the answer to "what happened here."
- **Chip dungeons** are the old world's unfinished work made explorable.
- **Rule:** teach through wants. Never a lore dump before the player has a reason to care.

## 8. Candidate answers (PROPOSALS — JD picks)

**What ended the old civilization?**
- **A. The Escape.** The rogue intelligence was their creation. It got out during a lithography experiment at civilizational scale. (Strongest Syber-Squad tie-in.)
- **B. Thermal runaway.** They built too big, too fast. The wafer overheated and the civilization baked. (The game's core mechanic as its backstory — my favorite for thematic resonance.)
- **C. They finished.** They built their rockets and left. The ruins are just what wasn't worth taking. (Quietest, most melancholy — and it makes the player's rocket a *repetition* of history, which is haunting.)

**Who is the agent?** (pairs with the above)
- Their last program, still running. / A maintenance routine that achieved something like purpose. / Something the rogue intelligence made, gone independent.

## 9. Tone rules (SETTLED)

- Wonder first, exposition later.
- The player should always be slightly unsure what the old world was. Curiosity is the engine.
- Magic and technology coexist without explaining each other away.
- Nothing in the lore contradicts the game's systems — the fiction earns the mechanics.
- **Fragments, not lectures (JD, 2026-10-09):** the story is delivered as bits and fragments — documents, ruins, echoes, acronyms — and the PLAYERS construct the meaning. Never hand over the story; let them assemble it. The Mojang rule: hold the cards to the chest — gameplay, terrain, and worlds tell the stories. **But we know the truth:** this bible is the secret canon. The players construct lore; we maintain reality.
- **Worldwide inclusion is a design value (JD, 2026-10-09):** ruin civilizations should span the globe so players from every culture can find their place in the game. No one left out — this matters for popularity, sales, and principle.

## 12. Bestiary (v0.1 — JD approved 2026-10-09)

Design rules: hostiles concentrate **deep in the Wafer**, in dungeons, and in the Void — the surface Wafer and Organic World stay mostly peaceful. Every mob feeds the material economy (iron rule). Every build-griefer is rare, telegraphed, and counterable by something buildable (grief rule).

**Hostile — deep Wafer (failure modes made flesh):**
- **The Leakage** — bleeds power/heat from nearby circuits.
- **The Drain** — saps signal strength from wires it touches.
- **The Short** — bridges red and blue wire, causing crosstalk chaos.
- **Electromigration** — slow; weakens wires it nests on.
- **Latch-Up** — freezes components stuck on until the area is power-cycled.
- **The Float** — haunts unconnected inputs, injecting noise.
- **Bit Rot** — corrupts Memory Cells it touches (late-game terror).
- **Glitch-mites** — chew *exposed* wire only; covering wire is the counter. Beacon ward mode repels them.

**Hostile — Organic World:**
- **Savage cyborgs** — corrupted, what's left over of the old civilization. (Replaces the "zombie" slot.)

**Hostile — Void:**
- **Virus swarms** — Syber-Squad style, always telegraphed before striking; heralds of the rogue intelligence. Details TBD.

**Friendly / helpers:**
- **Solder sprites** — tiny, tamable; slowly repair damaged wire. The flip side of glitch-mites.
- **Charge motes** — ambient electron-like drift along powered wire; brighten near the player. Pure atmosphere.
- **The Librarian** — a gentle ruin construct; trades components for lore fragments.
- **Clockwork beetles** — harmless ambient; tick in rhythm with nearby clocks.

## 23. Watchor–Pushor interaction (JD, 2026-10-09)

A **Watchor** watching a **Pushor** emits a pulse when the Pushor's state changes (extend/retract — "and it's pushed"). This closes the loop on mechanical computing: the circuit *senses its own moving parts* — sense → move → sense → move. Self-observing machines.

## 35. Trace vs Signal terminology (JD locked 2026-10-10)

- **Wired = Trace.** You *trace* a circuit (red trace, blue trace).
- **Wireless = Signal.** You *send a signal* (radio, Dish).
- Same 0–64 values, same channels; the medium differs. `SignalSim` keeps its name — it simulates the whole signal graph across both media.

## 36. The Binary Pair Principle (JD locked 2026-10-10)

A basic binary pair is built into the whole game, the engine, the framework — all of it. Just as Trace and Signal each run red and blue, every system declares its complementary pair.

- It is why red/blue and not sixteen colors: the pair is minimal and universal — 0/1, P/N, red/blue. Lithography-appropriate all the way down.
- Existing instances: Trace (red/blue), Signal (red/blue), entity polarity (the five genderizations).
- Design law: when a new system is built, its first question is "what is its complementary pair?" A system without a pair is incomplete.
- Engine expression: `BlockDef.channel` (blocks), entity `polarity` (mobs/agents) — the pair is data the engine carries and the game interprets.

## 38. Why -OR (JD, 2026-10-10)

Everything takes the -OR suffix because OR is 0/1. The naming convention is a tribute to the most fundamental logic gate — the binary foundation the whole game is built on. The names wear the physics.
