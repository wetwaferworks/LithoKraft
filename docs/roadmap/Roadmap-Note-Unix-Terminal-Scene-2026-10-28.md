## The idea (JD, dictated 2026-10-28 01:09 EDT)

Somewhere in LithoKraft there should be a Unix-like system... a
terminal the player has to activate... staged like THAT scene...
that the player reenacts. JD's words: "that would be super super
funny." Filed as breadcrumbs on the roadmap, per his instruction.

## The scene being honored

Jurassic Park (1993): Lex at the park's control-room workstation,
flying the 3D file-system browser to bring the park systems back
online... "This is a UNIX system! I know this!"

JD saw the film in 1993 with his father (a computer technician),
age nine or ten; his report tonight: amazed, utterly amazed, and
scared, in the best way. This note exists because that memory
walked into the room during the phone-framerate session (the
Muldoon "clever girl" beat, 2026-10-28 01:07 EDT).

## Placement (JD, 2026-10-28 01:10 EDT, minutes later)

JD placed it himself: **emerging into the Organic, at the
abandoned fab.** There is a computer screen there the player must
go to in order to unlock a room. The locked room holds items the
player needs... "like the glass or something, the HR components"
(JD's words). You have to hack this little Unix system to get in
and get them.

So the beat is load-bearing, not decorative: the reenactment
gates real progression materials inside the Organic's abandoned
fab.

## Gating (JD, 2026-10-28 01:11 EDT)

The room is surrounded by blocks the player **currently cannot
mine**... an obsidian-like block that does not break without the
right tools. Two routes in, by design:

1. **Early (the intended first visit):** hack the Unix terminal;
   the door unlocks. The reenactment IS the key.
2. **Late (earned brute force):** once the player has upgraded
   tools, they can simply mine their way in. No invisible walls,
   no fake rock: the wall was always honestly unbreakable-at-
   your-tier, and progression makes it breakable.

**The anchor analogy (JD, 2026-10-28 01:14 EDT):** the
abandoned fab is LithoKraft's **Nether Fortress**. In Minecraft
you cannot progress toward the end without a fortress: it
gates the materials the next tier is made of, inside the
hostile dimension you cannot comfortably live in. The fab is
the same shape in the Organic, and this room is its first
challenge.

JD's reasoning, in substance: it is a mining game, so the wall
must obey mining rules. The hack is not a locked door pasted
over a wall you should be able to dig through... the dig-through
exists, it just belongs to a later tool tier.

**First visit vs. later visits (JD, 2026-10-28 01:13 EDT):**
the FIRST time the player comes to this room, the barrier is
unavoidable... they have to reach the computer and figure out
the hacking challenge; there is no other way in yet. LATER,
when the player is exploring fabs and wants more of the item,
they can just mine their way in and avoid the challenge
completely. The reenactment is mandatory exactly once, then
skippable forever: a rite the first time, a farm route after.

## The wider law + the loop (JD, 2026-10-28 01:12 EDT)

JD, stated as a standing law for the whole game: "LithoKraft
has to be like Minecraft. It has to have multiple ways to do
multiple things. And mining should always be an option."

And the loop that makes the fab room sing: **the tools that can
mine that block are themselves in the Organic**... the dimension
the player just emerged into... but the player **cannot stay in
the Organic very long**; they have to go back to the Wafer and
build better stuff. So the fab visit runs on a clock: get in
(the hack), grab what you need, get home, build... and each
return trip stays longer and digs deeper. The terminal hack,
the dwell limit, and the tool tier are one mechanism wearing
three faces.

## Shape, as clay (nothing locked, nothing built)

- A derelict control room (a ruin? a dead dimension's facility?)
  holding an ancient terminal that runs a Unix-like system.
  (Superseded in part by the Placement above: the fab in the
  Organic is the home JD named.)
- Activation is a small reenactment, not a button press: the
  player flies a 3D file tree... directories as rooms/volumes,
  files as objects... to find and run the restore sequence that
  brings the place (power, doors, a dimension interface?) back
  online.
- The joke lands for the players who know; for everyone else it
  still plays as a discoverable, charming interaction. Homage,
  never appropriation: our terminal, our file tree, our words.
- Possible pairing (Elfie's suggestion, clay): a locked sibling
  terminal that refuses a wrong incantation with a wagging
  taunt... the "magic word" beat, in our own words.
- Fits standing doctrine: world permanence (the restored system
  STAYS restored), teaching through play (a real Unix mental
  model, hidden inside the joke), and JD's melting-pot mission
  (everything he ever watched goes in).
